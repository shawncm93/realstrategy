Who we work with

Four kinds of client make up most of the practice. Each has tax rules a general practitioner sees a few times a year and we see every week.

Real estate investors and agents

Rental losses, depreciation, and when a sale is taxable are decided by elections and records made during the year, so that is when we work on them.

  • Real estate professional status and material participation
  • Cost segregation, and what California does with it
  • 1031 exchange timing and replacement property
  • Agent commissions: S corporation, estimates, and vehicle deductions

Airbnb, Turo, and platform operators

Platform income arrives on 1099-Ks that rarely match your books. We reconcile it, apply the short-term rental rules correctly, and set estimates from the current year.

  • Short-term rental loss rules and the seven-day average
  • Vehicle depreciation and mileage for Turo fleets
  • Self-employment tax exposure and how to limit it
  • Quarterly estimates built from a live projection

Content creators

YouTube, sponsorships, affiliate income, and merch each report differently. We put them on one set of books and pick the structure that fits the income level.

  • When an S corporation pays off and when it does not
  • Home studio, equipment, and travel substantiation
  • 1099 income across platforms and brands
  • Multi-state filing when sponsors and audiences are everywhere

Founders and early-stage companies

Entity choice, officer pay, and equity decisions made in year one follow the company for years. We get them right before the first return is filed.

  • LLC, S corporation, or C corporation at formation
  • Reasonable compensation for shareholder-employees
  • ISO, NSO, and RSU planning for the founder's own return
  • Federal and California filing for a new entity

California has its own rules

Three differences that change the numbers for almost every client we see. Every projection we run is on both federal and California law.

  • No bonus depreciationCalifornia has never conformed to federal bonus depreciation. A cost segregation study that wipes out federal tax in year one still leaves California tax to pay, spread over the normal recovery periods.
  • No qualified business income deductionThe 20 percent federal deduction for pass-through income does not exist on the California return. S corporation and sole proprietor projections have to be run twice.
  • $800 minimum taxLLCs, S corporations, and corporations registered in California owe $800 a year to the Franchise Tax Board whether or not they made money, apart from a first-year exemption for newly formed corporations. It is often the first bill a new entity gets.

Clients outside California get the same treatment for their own state: the projection is run on that state's law as well as federal.
